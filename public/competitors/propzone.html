PropZone vs BidDeed.AI

PropZone sells zoning data as a SaaS product for developers and municipalities. They have solid UI and parcel visualization — but zero foreclosure intelligence, no ML pipeline, and no agentic architecture. Our 12 patent claims cover capabilities they cannot replicate without infringing.

Category: Zoning Data SaaS
Competitor Profile
PZ

PropZone

propzone.com · Zoning Data SaaS · US-based

PropZone provides parcel-level zoning data through a web interface and API. Their product allows developers to look up zoning designations, permitted uses, and development constraints for individual parcels. They serve municipalities, commercial developers, and real estate professionals who need to understand what can be built on a given site.

Their strength is clean UI and decent data coverage across multiple states. Their weakness: they are a pure data lookup tool with no investment intelligence, no auction pipeline, no ML scoring, and no multi-agent orchestration. They answer "what's the zoning?" — we answer "should you buy this property, at what price, and why?"

PropZone's Structural Weaknesses

No Foreclosure Intelligence

PropZone has zero integration with county clerk auction data. They cannot tell you which properties are going to auction, what the opening bid is, or what the judgment amount is. Our pipeline ingests 245K+ auction records across multiple counties.

No ML Deal Scoring

No ARV estimation, no max bid calculation, no BID/REVIEW/SKIP classification. PropZone shows you zoning — it cannot tell you whether a deal is profitable. Our XGBoost pipeline (Claim 8) scores every property automatically.

No Agentic Architecture

PropZone is a traditional web app with a static database. No autonomous agents, no self-healing scrapers, no adaptive monitoring. Our 14-agent orchestration (Claim 1) operates continuously without human intervention.

No Cross-Auction Intelligence

PropZone cannot correlate foreclosure (FC) and tax deed (TD) data for the same parcel. Our Cross-Auction Intelligence system (Claim 9) identifies dual-exposure properties that represent asymmetric opportunities.
